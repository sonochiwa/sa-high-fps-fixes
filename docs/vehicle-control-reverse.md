# Vehicle control: reverse-engineering map

What decides how a player-driven vehicle turns, where each step depends on
the rendered frame rate, and which of those steps a fix may touch without
changing anything else. Addresses are GTA SA 1.0 US; every site below was
read from the executable disassembly, not from a decompiler.

Companion to `docs/vehicle-physics-audit.md` (tyre and body friction, why the
1.0.0 driving reset happened) and `docs/bike-physics-reverse.md` (bike
suspension, pitch and landing). This note covers the part neither of those
did: the yaw chain, the steering inputs and the aircraft side of the one
function all of them share.

## Method

- `gta-reversed` at `ead3051a` (2026-09-16) supplies the parts it has
  recovered: `CPhysical`, `CAutomobile::ProcessControl`,
  `CAutomobile::ProcessControlInputs`, `CVehicle::ProcessWheel`. Nothing in
  its vehicle physics changed since `5fad52b9`.
- `CVehicle::FlyingControl`, `CBike::ProcessControl`,
  `CBike::ProcessControlInputs`, `CPlane::ProcessControlInputs`,
  `CHeli::ProcessControlInputs`, `CQuadBike::ProcessControl` and
  `CMonsterTruck::ProcessControl` are still stubs there. They were read from
  a `dumpbin /disasm` listing of the executable with
  `references/tools/annotate-function.py`, which resolves every `ds:[addr]`
  constant to its value and names `this` fields from the plugin-sdk offset
  tables. Bytes quoted below are from the Hoodlum build; Compact shares the
  layout at every site named here.
- `ratio` means `CTimer::ms_fTimeStep / (50/30)`: exactly `1.0` at 30 FPS.

Per-frame quantities are sorted into the five kinds the audit defines (decay
factor, rate, impulse, budget, constraint). Only the first is a frame-rate bug
when the timestep is missing; a rate or impulse that already carries
`ms_fTimeStep` is correct as written.

## The yaw chain of a driven car

Per frame, in order, for a `CAutomobile` under `STATUS_PLAYER`:

| Step | Address | What it does | Timestep | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `0x6AD831`–`0x6AD935` `ProcessControlInputs` | `m_fRawSteerAngle += (−pad/128 − raw) · ts · 0.2` (two sites, see below); mouse path `raw −= dx · 0.0035`, return `raw *= pow(0.975, ts)` | rate, linear step of an exponential | 7% slow at high FPS; fixable, low value |
| 2 | `0x6ADCDB`–`0x6ADD1E` | `m_fSteerAngle = lock° · raw·|raw|` | none | clean |
| 3 | `0x6B33F6` region `ProcessControl` | limiter: `m_fSteerAngle *= asin(min(1, adhesion·traction·16 / v²)) / lock` | none, `m_fVelocityFrequency` is a constant `1.0` | clean |
| 4 | `ProcessCarWheelPair` → `CVehicle::ProcessWheel` `0x6D6C00` | lateral slip cancel per wheel, applied as move + turn impulse at the contact point; `adhesion *= ts` at `0x6D6C7B` | budget vs velocity | see audit; do not touch |
| 5 | `CPhysical::ProcessControl` `0x5485E0` → `ApplyFriction()` | adds the friction accumulators; bike-on-side decay `pow(0.5, ts)` | correct | clean |
| 6 | `ApplyAirResistance` `0x544C40`, site `0x544D29` | `m_vecTurnSpeed *= 0.99` **per rendered frame** | **none** | the yaw drain; the only decay factor in the chain without a timestep |
| 7 | `ApplyTurnSpeed` / `ApplyMoveSpeed` | integrate | `· ts` | clean |

Step 6 is the whole story of "the car turns heavily above 30 FPS". Steps 4
and 6 are the only two places that subtract angular momentum from a car on
its wheels; step 4 is scale-consistent per second (the audit's analysis; the
slip it cancels is regenerated by the body's motion and so is itself
proportional to `ts` in steady cornering), step 6 is not.

Retention of yaw rate over one second, stock: `0.99^FPS`.

| FPS | Retained | Relative to 30 FPS |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 30 | 0.740 | 1.00 |
| 60 | 0.547 | 0.74 |
| 100 | 0.366 | 0.49 |
| 144 | 0.235 | 0.32 |
| 300 | 0.049 | 0.07 |

### `CPhysical::ApplyAirResistance`, verified from the executable

```

00544C55: jne  00544CA2             ; > 0.1 → vehicle-or-not test
00544C57: mov  al,[esi+36h] ; and al,7 ; cmp al,2   ; entity type VEHICLE
00544C5E: je   00544CA2
  ; non-vehicle with m_fAirResistance > 0.1:
00544C66: fld  ms_fTimeStep ; call pow ; move *= ; turn *=     ← both correct
00544CA2: ; vehicles, and anything with m_fAirResistance <= 0.1:

00544CFE: fld 1.0 ; fsub ; fld ms_fTimeStep ; call pow
00544D13: move.x/y/z *= st                                     ← correct
00544D29: fld [esi+50h] ; fmul ds:[00862CD0h] (0.99) ; fstp [esi+50h]
00544D35: fld [esi+54h] ; fmul ds:[00862CD0h]        ; fstp [esi+54h]
00544D41: fld [esi+58h] ; fmul ds:[00862CD0h]        ; fstp [esi+58h]
00544D4D: pop esi ; pop ecx ; ret
```

Bytes at `0x544D29`, 36 of them, the span the 0.9.x `turnAirResistance`
thunk replaced:

```
D9 46 50 D8 0D D0 2C 86 00 D9 5E 50
D9 46 54 D8 0D D0 2C 86 00 D9 5E 54
D9 46 58 D8 0D D0 2C 86 00 D9 5E 58
```

`esi` is `this` for the whole function. Called only from
`CPhysical::ProcessControl`, which every vehicle class runs once per frame:
`CAutomobile::ProcessControl` (physics and simple paths), `CBike`, `CBoat`,
`CTrain`; `CPlane`, `CHeli`, `CQuadBike`, `CMonsterTruck`, `CTrailer` reach
it through `CAutomobile`. So a patch at this site reaches **every** vehicle,
which is what the 0.9.x fix did and why it changed aircraft.

Field offsets a scoped patch needs, all confirmed in the listing:

| Field | Offset | Confirmed at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| entity type | `[this+0x36] & 7`, `2` = vehicle | `0x544C57` |
| `m_nVehicleType` (base class) | `+0x590` | `0x6D8E05` |
| `m_nVehicleSubType` (leaf class) | `+0x594` | `0x544CE1`, `0x6D96D2` |
| `CAutomobile::m_nNumContactWheels` | `+0x960` | `0x6B26BF` (zeroed), `0x6B2717` (counted), `0x6B520B` (simple path) in `ProcessControl` |
| `CBike::m_nNumContactWheels` | `+0x804` | `0x6BA6EE` |

Subtype values: 0 automobile, 1 monster truck, 2 quad, 3 heli, 4 plane,
5 boat, 6 train, 7/8 unused flying types, 9 bike, 10 BMX, 11 trailer.

`m_nNumContactWheels` is written later in the same frame than
`ApplyAirResistance` runs (the wheel pass comes after
`CPhysical::ProcessControl`), so a thunk reads the previous frame's count.
One frame of lag on a gate that only selects a damping constant is harmless.

## Steering input slew: every site

All four player vehicle classes shape keyboard/pad steering with the same
linear step of an exponential approach. The mouse paths beside them are
already `pow(k, ts)`.

| Class | Function | Site | Bytes | Field | Constant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| car | `CAutomobile::ProcessControlInputs` `0x6AD690` | `0x6AD864` | `D8 4C 24 18 D8 0D 58 10 87 00` | `m_fRawSteerAngle` `+0x58C` | `0x871058` = 0.2 |
| car | same | `0x6AD925` | `D8 0D 58 10 87 00 D8 4C 24 18` | same (operands swapped) | same |
| bike | `CBike::ProcessControlInputs` `0x6BE310` | `0x6BE4A7` | `D8 4C 24 18 D8 0D 00 13 87 00` | `m_fRawSteerAngle` | `0x871300` = 0.2 |
| bike | same | `0x6BE5F8` | `D8 4C 24 18 D8 0D 00 13 87 00` | same | same |
| bike | same | `0x6BE63F` | `D8 4C 24 14 D8 0D C4 8C 85 00` | `CRideAnimData::LeanFwd` `+0x650` | `0x858CC4` = 0.2 |
| plane | `CPlane::ProcessControlInputs` `0x6CADD0` | `0x6CAF2C`, `0x6CB06C`, `0x6CB0B1`, `0x6CB1AD` | `D8 4C 24 18|14 D8 0D C4 8C 85 00` | `+0x990` / `+0x98C` (left-right, up-down), keyboard and pad branches | `0x858CC4` = 0.2 |
| heli | `CHeli::ProcessControlInputs` `0x6C4830` | — | — | direct set, no slew | — |

`[esp+18h]` / `[esp+14h]` hold a copy of `ms_fTimeStep` made a few
instructions earlier (`0x6AD831`, `0x6AD8F0`, `0x6BE46A`, `0x6BE4BD`,
`0x6BE5C4`, `0x6BE60E`). Each site is a ten byte pair `fmul [esp+N]` /
`fmul ds:[k]` with `(target − current)` on `st(0)`, so one thunk shape fits
all of them: replace the two multiplies by `· (1 − pow(1 − k·(50/30), ratio))`.
At 30 FPS that is `k · 50/30 = 1/3`, bit-for-bit what the two multiplies
produce.

Effect size, retention of the steering error per 1/30 s:

| FPS | Retained |
| ---: | ---: |
| 30 | 0.667 |
| 60 | 0.694 |
| 144 | 0.708 |
| ∞ | 0.717 |

So full lock arrives about 7% later at high FPS. Real, in the "heavier"
direction, and small. Keyboard input itself is a step to ±128
(`CControllerConfigManager::AffectPadFromKeyBoard`), so this slew is the
only shaping between the key and `m_fRawSteerAngle`.

The bike `LeanFwd` site and the plane sites are listed for completeness;
neither is part of the ground handling fix.

## `CVehicle::FlyingControl` `0x6D85F0`

Not reversed in `gta-reversed`. Scanned in full (2 916 instructions: every
write to a `this` field and every `ApplyMoveForce` / `ApplyTurnForce` call
checked for the timestep in its own computation; the model-0 block, the entry
of the plane and heli blocks and the resistance tail read by hand) to answer
one question: does the flight code contain its own frame-rate dependence that
the stock `0.99` per frame was masking? It does not.

Structure: a switch on the flight model via the table at `0x6DAEE4`:

| Model | Block | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `0x6D8715` | simple model (`FLIGHT_MODEL_UNK` in gta-reversed): yaw torque from `m_fSteerAngle`, `turn.y *= pow(0.9, ts)` at `0x6D8B01` |
| 1, 3, 4, 5 | `0x6D8B1D` | plane, flying car, boat |
| 2, 6, 8 | `0x6D986E` | RC, heli, hydra |
| 7 | `0x6DA900` | falls straight to the resistance tail |

All blocks end at the resistance tail `0x6DA900`–`0x6DAEE1`:

```
006DA906: fld [edi+40h]  ; vecTurnRes.x  ; fld ms_fTimeStep ; call pow
006DA930: fld [edi+44h]  ; vecTurnRes.y  ; fld ms_fTimeStep ; call pow
006DA942: fld [edi+48h]  ; vecTurnRes.z  ; fld ms_fTimeStep ; call pow
006DAA0B..: per axis, either 1/(speedRes·ω²+1)·turnRes raised to ts, applied as
            an impulse (turnSpeed·mult − turnSpeed)·turnMass via ApplyTurnForce
            at 0x6DAC11 / 0x6DACE3 / 0x6DADB5, or the plain pow'd factor
```

Force applications, all 28 of them, checked individually for a
`ms_fTimeStep` multiply in their own computation (between them and the
previous force call):

| Block | `ApplyMoveForce` | `ApplyTurnForce` | Missing timestep |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| model 0 | `0x6D889C`, `0x6D8ACF` | `0x6D883E`, `0x6D88DB`, `0x6D89BE`, `0x6D8AFC` | none |
| plane | `0x6D8DB2`, `0x6D9124`, `0x6D91EA`, `0x6D9864` | `0x6D9348`, `0x6D9412`, `0x6D956D`, `0x6D966A` | none |
| heli | `0x6D99D9`, `0x6D9E05`, `0x6D9EF4`, `0x6DA74A` | `0x6DA068`, `0x6DA1D3`, `0x6DA26F`, `0x6DA58F`, `0x6DA674`, `0x6DA816`, `0x6DA8FB` | none |
| tail | — | `0x6DAC11`, `0x6DACE3`, `0x6DADB5` | derived from the pow'd factors above |

Direct velocity writes: `turn.y *= pow(0.9, ts)` (`0x6D8B15`, model 0) and
`move.y/z *= pow(fMoveRes, ts)` (`0x6D989B`/`0x6D98A1`, heli). Rotor lift
state at `+0x84C` ramps with `ts · 0.006` and decays with `pow(0.997, ts)`.
The roll timer at `+0x9A0` (`0x6D9702`) is the site `vehicleTimers` already
carries.

Conclusion: an aircraft's angular damping per second is
`vecTurnRes^50 · 0.99^FPS`. The first factor is handling data applied
correctly; the second is the same bug as the car's. At 300 FPS the second
factor alone is `0.99^270 ≈ 0.066` smaller than at 30 FPS. Removing it
globally hands a Hydra or a Maverick fifteen times more angular momentum per
second than a high-FPS player has ever flown with. That is the "amplified
flight physics" of 0.9.x: the 30 FPS aircraft, not a wrong formula. It is
also why the fix must be scoped by vehicle class rather than merely tuned.

`CPlane::ProcessControl` `0x6C9260`, `CPlane::ProcessFlyingCarStuff`
`0x6CB7C0` (12 timestep uses, three `FlyingControl` calls, no velocity
writes), `CHeli::ProcessFlyingCarStuff` `0x6C4E60` (rotor, covered by
`heliRotorSpeed`) contain no further decay factors.

## `CBike::ProcessControl` `0x6B9250`: the steering and lean sub-chain

`docs/bike-physics-reverse.md` maps the suspension and pitch path. The
remaining state writes in the function, from a full scan of every
`fstp [this+…]`:

| Site | Field | Shape | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `0x6B9B70`–`0x6B9B82` | `BarSteerAngle` | riderless bike on stand: `+= ts · 1°` toward 20° | rate, clean |
| `0x6B9B90`–`0x6B9C2F` | `DesiredLeanAngle`, `LeanAngle` | riderless: `pow(0.97, ts)` blend toward the ground lean | decay, clean (`bikeLeanTarget` territory) |
| `0x6B9C82`–`0x6B9CB5` | `m_fSteerAngle` | riderless: `±ts · 0.5°` toward centre, capped at ±25° | rate, clean |
| `0x6B9D51` | `m_fAirResistance` | ×0.85 / ×0.6 while the rider leans forward (a state, rewritten each frame from handling) | not a decay |
| `0x6BAA1E`–`0x6BAA30` | `BarSteerAngle` | crashed/knocked-off: `+= ts · 1.5°` | rate, clean |
| `0x6BAA6C`–`0x6BAA83` | `BarSteerAngle` | stationary, no steer input: `*= pow(0.96, ts)` | decay, clean |
| `0x6BAA88`–`0x6BAC1C` | `BarSteerAngle` | `= m_fSteerAngle · min(1, asin(min(1, adhesion·fSpeedSteer·traction·4 / v²)) / lock)`, doubled when steer and lean agree, `fSlipSteer` on loose surfaces | no timestep, the bike's version of the car limiter | clean |
| `0x6BAC77`–`0x6BAC8F` | `m_aWheelAngularVelocity[0]`, pitch | both wheels off the ground: `*= 0.95` per frame | decay without timestep | cosmetic (wheel spin), already the `bikeWheelSpin` family |
| `0x6BB99A`–`0x6BBBAD` | `DesiredLeanAngle`, `LeanAngle` | lean target from lateral acceleration (`bikeLeanTarget` site) | covered | — |
| `0x6BC66F`–`0x6BC681` | `LeanAngle` | rider lean follows bar steer, `· ts` | rate, clean |

So on a bike, as on a car, the only yaw-side decay without a timestep is the
shared `0.99` in `ApplyAirResistance`; the bike's own steering chain is
frame-clean.

`CBike::ProcessControlInputs` `0x6BE310`, read in full: keyboard slew as
tabled above; mouse `raw −= dx · 0.0035`, `LeanFwd −= dy · 0.0035`; return
to centre `pow(0.98, ts)` when |raw| > 0.35 or no input; then
`m_fSteerAngle = lock° · raw·|raw|`, gas/brake, comedy controls keyed on
`m_snTimeInMilliseconds` (wall clock, fine).

## Other subclasses

- `CQuadBike::ProcessControl` `0x6CDCC0`: two `ApplyTurnForce` calls
  (`0x6CDF5B`, `0x6CDFFE`), both impulses of the form
  `(ω · pow(k, ts) − ω) · turnMass`, the same construction as the resistance
  tail of `FlyingControl` and the hydraulics yaw damp in
  `CAutomobile::ProcessControl`. Correct. Then `CAutomobile::ProcessControl`.
- `CMonsterTruck::ProcessControl` `0x6C8250`: no timestep, no forces; a
  wrapper.
- `CAutomobile::ProcessControl` in-air control (`0x6B4F69` onward):
  turn forces `· ts`, gated on `|ω·axis| < 0.02`. Frame-clean in
  themselves; what a high-FPS player experiences as "stable in the air" is
  the `0.99` drain against these.
- Hydraulics yaw damp (`0x6B30E0`): `pow(0.985, ts)` and a second `pow`
  on top; impulse form. Correct.
- `DoSoftGroundResistance`: `· ts · mass`. Correct.

## Non-findings

Checked and cleared, so nobody re-audits them:

- `m_fVelocityFrequency` is set to `1.0` in the `CPhysical` constructor and
  never changed; the steer limiter and `traction` in
  `CAutomobile::ProcessControl` are frame-independent.
- `CControllerConfigManager` writes ±128 straight into the stick; there is no
  keyboard ramp before the slew above.
- `CPhysical::ApplyFriction()` (accumulator form) has no per-frame constant
  on a vehicle.
- `CPad::NewMouseControllerState` deltas are per frame by nature; summing
  them at any frame rate gives the same travel.

## What the telemetry added

The map above was checked against a throwaway telemetry plugin (one row per
frame: inputs, speeds, angles, wheel states, suspension compression, the
turn forces applied) driving a scripted input sequence on a flat collision
box 1400 m up, once with the game's limiter off and once at 30 FPS, three
vehicles (Elegy, Infernus, NRG-500). Four things the map did not predict
came out of it; each was then read back from the executable.

### Gear change kick

`CAutomobile::ProcessControl` `0x6B24D6`–`0x6B268E` applies
`ApplyTurnForce(up · −0.008 · min(turnMass, 2500), −forward)` on every
frame that `bAudioChangingGear` is set. The audio entity sets the flag from
`CAEVehicleAudioEntity::JustFinishedAccelerationLoop` (`0x4F5E50`) once
`LoopFrameCnt` (`0x8CBCC0`, 10) frames have passed since the last time,
so above 30 FPS the kick repeats every eleventh frame for as long as the
sound sits at its end: seven to nine pitches of 21 degrees a second with
the front wheels lifting. `gearChangeKick` rewrites the counter each frame
to the number of frames that make up ten original ones.

### Wheel slip budget

`CVehicle::ProcessWheel` compares the slip it wants to cancel with
`adhesion · ms_fTimeStep`, a per-frame budget, but forms the lateral slip
(`0x6D6D2C`) and the off-throttle longitudinal slip (`0x6D6E3B`) as plain
velocities. Above 30 FPS a cornering tyre exceeds the shrunken budget on
every frame, is classed as skidding and loses `fTractionLoss` for the whole
corner. `wheelSlipRate` multiplies both velocities by `ratio` where they are
formed. Bike sites (`0x6D74F3`, `0x6D7610`) are left alone: with the car
fixes in place the NRG-500 measured within 5–10% of 30 FPS in every block.

### Suspension damping: the same-frame bite

The order of a car's frame is `CPhysical::ProcessControl` (friction,
gravity, air resistance), `ProcessSuspension` (springs, then dampers per
wheel), `ProcessCarWheelPair` (tyre forces), then the move in
`ProcessCollision`. `ApplySpringDampening` removes
`min(ms_fTimeStep · dampingLevel, 0.25)` of the contact point's speed
along the spring *as it stands when it runs*, which is the spring's reaction
to the previous frame's tyre forces; this frame's tyre impulse has not been
added. In a steady corner the body settles where the spring cancels the
tyre torque only after the damper's bite: `spring = tyre / (1 − d)`. At 30
FPS `d` is 0.25 for any damping level of 0.15 or more, so the body leans a
third further under a cornering or braking load than the spring rate
alone allows; at 1000 FPS `d` is 0.009 and the lean is the plain spring
answer. Ride height is exempt because gravity is applied before the springs
and the two cancel before the damper sees them.

Measured on the Elegy in a handbrake slide, steady phase: roll 2.25° at 30
FPS against 1.90° at 1000, pitch 1.45° against 1.15°, front-left
compression 0.56 against 0.59. Grip per wheel in `ProcessCarWheelPair` is
scaled by `1 − compression`, so the smaller weight transfer left the yaw
rate 3–7% short in every slide, and it was the last systematic difference.
Stock at 960 FPS, for scale: roll 0.71°, yaw 23°/s against 95 at 30 FPS.

`suspensionLoadLean` reproduces the bite. `CPhysical::ProcessControl`
(`0x5485E0`) is wrapped to record the entity's move and turn speed at the
start of its frame; the damper wrapper then removes, before the stock
arithmetic, `d30 − d` of the axial speed change the frame has made so far
at that contact point, applied through `CPhysical::ApplyForce` with the
game's own point mass and capped by the spring force as the stock damping
is. The stock no-reversal clamp is not applied to the bite: it compares
against the whole contact speed, and one frame's spring change at a high
frame rate is far below the body's residual motion, so the clamp swallowed
most of the bite (roll 1.96° with it, 2.35° without, target 2.25°). At 30
FPS the two fractions are equal and nothing is added.

### A stationary bike does not fall

A bike created or placed in the air with no input hangs there at high FPS
until it gets throttle or brake; at 30 FPS it drops at once. Reproduced
with the plugin removed at 960 FPS (`dropcheck z0=1404.91 z1=1404.91`), so
it is the game's own behaviour, not a regression. Not chased further: it
needs a bike at rest in the air, which play does not produce. The test
script works around it with a throttle blip before measuring the resting
height.

## The fix, as shipped in 1.3.0

Every switch derives from a site above. Sites live in
`src/modules/vehicle_control_addresses.h`, the factor and gain functions in
`src/modules/vehicles.cpp`, the installers in
`src/modules/installers/vehicle_control_installers.cpp`.

| Switch | Site | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `turnAirResistance` (+`Strength`) | `0x544D29` | `0.99` per frame → `pow(0.99, 1 + (ratio − 1) · strength)` for cars and bikes with a wheel on the ground; stock for aircraft, boats and airborne vehicles |
| `steerInputRate` | `0x6AD864`, `0x6AD925`, `0x6BE4A7`, `0x6BE5F8` | `· ts · k` → `· (1 − pow(1 − k · (50/30), ratio))` |
| `gearChangeInertia` | `0x6D087E`, `0x6D08E4` | engine inertia delta `· 1/ratio`; smoother `0.85` → `pow(0.85, ratio)` |
| `gearChangeKick` | `0x8CBCC0` | `LoopFrameCnt` rewritten each frame to `round(10 / ratio)` |
| `suspensionDampingLimit` | `0x543E90` | damping level rewritten so the per-frame removal equals the capped 30 FPS removal spread over the frame |
| `suspensionLoadLean` | `0x5485E0`, `0x543E90` | the damper's 30 FPS bite of the frame's own spring change, see above |
| `wheelSlipRate` | `0x6D6D2C`, `0x6D6E3B` | lateral and off-throttle longitudinal slip `· ratio` |

All of them are exact no-ops at 30 FPS: every factor is `1`, every
fraction equals the stock one and every counter equals its stock value when
`ratio` is `1`.

Measured result (`v15`: Elegy, Infernus and NRG-500, each once with the
limiter off at 1000–1200 FPS and once at 30 FPS, on the box): the mean yaw
rate of every block is within 5% of the 30 FPS pass for both cars and
within 7% for the bike, with two exceptions that are not physics. The
bike's lift-off handbrake drift is 10% faster above 30 FPS, unchanged by
any of the car fixes; its `ProcessBikeWheel` sites are out of scope. The
300 ms steering taps read 13–18% less yaw above 30 FPS in the log, but the
30 FPS rows are stamped with the start of their frame while holding the
state after it, so that pass runs one frame ahead on every edge; shifted
by that frame the steer and yaw curves of the two passes coincide, and on
screen both frame rates show the same thing at the same time. Before the
1.3.0 fixes the same cars read 1.5–5 times less yaw above 30 FPS.

## Still not reversed

- `CVehicle::ProcessBikeWheel` `0x6D73B0` lateral path (the bike note covers
  pitch). Expected to mirror `ProcessWheel`; left alone because the bike
  measured within 5–10% of 30 FPS with the car fixes only.
- Why a bike at rest in the air stays there above 30 FPS (previous section).
- `CVehicle::ProcessWheel` off-throttle threshold: understood, deliberately
  left alone (audit).
- `CBoat::ProcessControl` and the boat branch of `FlyingControl`: boats also
  get the `0.99` per frame; excluded from the fix by scope, not because they
  were shown to be clean.
